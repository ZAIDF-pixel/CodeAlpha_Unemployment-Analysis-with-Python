# 📊 Unemployment Analysis with Python

An end-to-end data analysis project investigating unemployment trends, regional disparities, and the economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic using Python.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Unemployment is a key economic indicator that reflects the overall health of an economy. This project analyzes dataset records of unemployment rates across various states/regions to uncover seasonal patterns, regional variations, and the immediate impact of COVID-19 lockdown measures in 2020.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- **Data Cleaning & Preprocessing:** Clean whitespace, handle missing values, and parse datetime features.
- **COVID-19 Impact Assessment:** Measure and compare unemployment rates before vs. during the peak lockdown period (March–June 2020).
- **Regional Analysis:** Identify states and regions hardest hit by economic shutdowns.
- **Seasonal & Monthly Trends:** Explore monthly distribution patterns to evaluate recovery trajectories.
- **Demographic Split:** Compare unemployment across Rural vs. Urban areas.
- **Policy Insights:** Derive actionable data-driven recommendations to inform economic and social policies.

---

## 📁 Datasets Used

1. `Unemployment.csv`: Contains regional and area-wise (Rural/Urban) unemployment metrics.
2. `Unemployment_Rate_upto_11_2020.csv`: Time-series data capturing monthly unemployment rates across Indian states up to November 2020.

---

## 🛠️ Tech Stack & Libraries

- **Language:** Python 3.x
- **Data Manipulation:** `pandas`, `numpy`
- **Data Visualization:** `matplotlib`, `seaborn`

---

## ⚙️ Key Findings & Insights

1. **COVID-19 Lockdown Surge:** Unemployment experienced a sharp spike during the height of lockdowns (April–May 2020) compared to pre-pandemic baseline averages.
2. **Regional Disparities:** Significant variation exists among regions/states, highlighting areas that require prioritized economic relief.
3. **Urban vs. Rural Dynamics:** Urban centers faced distinct structural employment challenges compared to rural regions during lockdown phases.
